Zero-based budgeting is a method where every dollar of income is assigned a specific job before you spend it. At the end of the month, your income minus your expenses should equal zero—not because you spent everything, but because every dollar was intentionally allocated.

## Why zero-based budgeting works

Traditional budgeting often fails because people track spending after the fact. Zero-based budgeting flips that: you plan first, then spend within those limits.

**Key benefits:**

- You always know where your money is going
- Overspending in one category forces a conscious trade-off elsewhere
- It builds awareness of spending habits quickly

## How to start your first budget

1. **List your monthly income** — salary, freelance, side income, everything.
2. **List fixed expenses** — rent, utilities, subscriptions, loan payments.
3. **Assign the rest** — groceries, dining out, savings, fun money, emergency fund.
4. **Track as you go** — log each expense against its category during the month.
5. **Adjust next month** — review what worked and reallocate.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Forgetting irregular expenses (annual insurance, car maintenance)
- Being too restrictive and giving up after one bad week
- Not reviewing and adjusting categories each month

Start simple. Three to five categories is enough for your first month. Add more as you get comfortable.
